About

Health data scientist working across clinical prediction, medical imaging and reconstruction, healthcare process improvement, and AI in emergency and post-acute care, with an eye for models clinicians can actually trust and use.

My path started at the lab bench: a degree in Medical Laboratory Technology, four years as a Senior Product Specialist in diagnostics technology, and time as a laboratory technologist. That clinical grounding still shapes how I approach data science today: I care less about squeezing out marginal accuracy than about models that are explainable, reproducible, and clinically defensible.

Since completing an MSc in Diagnostics, Data and Digital Health at the University of Warwick, I've worked across lecturing, research coordination, and digital health project coordination at KPJ Healthcare University, on projects spanning clinical prediction models, medical imaging and CT reconstruction, hospital process improvement, and AI-driven tools (including speech technology) for emergency and rehabilitation care. Always keen to connect with others working across health data, clinical analytics, and AI in healthcare.

AKI Prediction in COVID-19 Patients
Analysed 179,824 clinical and laboratory records from over 450 COVID-19 patients at a UK NHS teaching hospital to predict the onset and stage of acute kidney injury. Benchmarked six ML algorithms; XGBoost performed best, reaching 0.91 AUC for severe Stage 3 AKI, and used SHAP to show creatinine, urea, age, and gender as the strongest predictors, with sodium emerging as a useful alternative when creatinine data was missing.

Stroke Risk Prediction
Built a stroke risk classifier on 4,981 patient records across 10 clinical and demographic variables, addressing severe class imbalance (only 5% of patients had a stroke) using RandomOverSampler. Compared five algorithms; Random Forest performed best, reaching 99.2% accuracy with zero false negatives on the test set, prioritising sensitivity so no at-risk patient was missed.

98.6%recall (ResNet50)
Chest X-ray Pneumonia Detection
Built an automated pneumonia classifier for paediatric chest X-rays across 5,856 images, comparing custom CNNs against pre-trained ResNet50, AlexNet, and DenseNet-201. Used data augmentation and 5-fold cross-validation for robustness; ResNet50 performed best at 96.3% accuracy and 98.6% recall, prioritising recall to keep false negatives, and missed diagnoses, to a minimum. Also examined the cost-benefit and ethical questions around deploying such a tool in the NHS, including data privacy and algorithmic bias.

Limited-Data CT Reconstruction of a Pharmaceutical Tablet
Simulated reduced X-ray CT projections, down to 5% of the Nyquist number, on an ibuprofen tablet to study the trade-off between scan time and image quality. Compared FDK, CGLS, and SIRT reconstruction algorithms: CGLS best preserved edge detail under sparse data, while SIRT gave more consistent bead intensity for predicting drug release and dosage properties.

79.28%ED process capability (vs 95% target)
Emergency Department Performance Improvement
Evaluated emergency department performance at a 1,000-bed hospital against the UK's 4-hour ED and 90/90 stroke care quality indicators. Used process mapping, Spearman's rank correlation, and SPC charts in MATLAB to calculate an ED process capability of 79.28% against a 95% target, identifying ambulance and walk-in arrivals, plus limited bed availability, as the main drivers of extended wait times, some exceeding 700 minutes. Proposed data-driven interventions, including specialised care pathways and an ED–Radiology pre-alert system.
